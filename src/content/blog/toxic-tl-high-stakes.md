# Surviving High-Stakes Deadlines Under Toxic Tech Leadership

Engineering under pressure is hard. Engineering under pressure with a Team Lead who uses condescension as a management style is a different level of exhausting.

As the deadline approached, I could sense the urgency coming from the TL. Every day started to feel like firefighting. The work itself was difficult, but the pressure was made worse by how it was managed.

---

## 1. The Red Flags

The pattern became clear:

- **Urgency without direction:** The deadline pressure was constant, but it made each day feel reactive instead of focused.
- **Condescension as a management style:** Questions and mistakes became opportunities to talk down to people rather than help them move forward.
- **Information routed through one person:** The TL positioned himself as the path for all information. That created a bottleneck and made ordinary collaboration harder than it needed to be.

When leadership relies on pressure and condescension, communication doesn't improve—it shuts down. People spend energy managing the leader instead of solving the problem.

---

## 2. How We Survived (And Still Delivered)

We couldn't change the TL's behavior mid-deadline, so we worked around the friction without letting it define how we worked:

1. **Collaborate with people you work well with:** We built working relationships directly with teammates we trusted, rather than waiting for every interaction to pass through the TL.
2. **Boost each other:** We backed each other up, shared encouragement, and kept one another moving when the pressure started to wear us down.

That didn't make the leadership problem disappear. It gave the team enough support to keep doing the work.

---

## 3. What I Took Away From It

- **Urgency is not leadership.** A deadline can be real without turning every day into a crisis. Good leadership converts pressure into priorities and decisions.
- **Information bottlenecks slow teams down.** When one person controls every connection, the team loses speed and context.
- **Condescension is not a high standard.** It makes people less likely to ask questions, surface risks, or learn in public.
- **Peer support can protect delivery, but it should not be the safety net.** Teams can lift each other up, but leaders are responsible for creating the conditions that make that support possible.

High stakes don't justify bad behavior. Panic is contagious, but so is calm—and calm is a leadership choice.
